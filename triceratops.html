01 · Overview

Students want to join the hunt, but the club's information is scattered across Discord

When students discover UC Davis Triceratops, a campus club built around crochet dinosaur scavenger hunts, they're met with a 41-channel Discord server and rules spread across it. Many follow the Instagram but never learn the rules or how to get started, so interested students stall before they ever join.

The club hired our team of 6 designer-developers to design and build a website that turns that scattered information into one clear first step. I owned the onboarding pages: Getting Started and Flash Sales.

02 · Problem

Discord was the only way in, and new members got lost in it

“I followed the Instagram but I didn't know you had to go on Discord to find drop info.”Interview participant
Annotated screenshot: the old DiscordThe 41-channel server with 2–3 pain points called out

Designed forStudents who'd never heard of the club, and interested students who hadn't joined Discord yet. Active hunters were a secondary audience.

03 · Constraints

I had to learn to build the site while I was designing it

  • New to code. No backend or Claude Code experience going in.
  • No usage data. Discord couldn't show where newcomers dropped off, so I relied on surveys and interviews.
  • 18 weeks to design and ship, while learning GitHub and Cloudflare.
04 · Insight

Rules, not discovery, were the biggest barrier to joining

62-response survey · 12 interviews · mid-fi usability test with 6 students · affinity mapping, IA and a SWOT

9 of 15

interested students said not knowing the rules stopped them from joining.

Testing the mid-fi showed where it still failed

  1. 3 of 6couldn't find what to do after claiming a dino. They wanted “what to do and what to do after.”
  2. 2 of 6mentioned scroll fatigue.
  3. 2 of 6missed the notification steps.
05 · Key decisions

I cut what overwhelmed new members, and kept what guided them

1Cut the rulebook into 4 scannable sections

Rejected Showing everything the client gave us. Mentors called it congested, and 2 of 6 testers had scroll fatigue.

07 · Reflections

I learned to design for what new members need first, not everything they could know

My first versions showed everything the client gave us. Testers just wanted “what to do and what to do after,” so reducing friction meant cutting, not explaining more.

I learned that accessibility is a design decision, not a final check

A failing contrast ratio and competing GIFs slipped into my designs. Now I plan for contrast, motion and readability from the first draft, instead of patching them at the end.

08 · The outcome

Our onboarding pages shipped, approved by the client!

After QA, my design lead and the client approved the final pages, and they're live on the club's site. Mentors called Getting Started the strongest visual direction of the project.
