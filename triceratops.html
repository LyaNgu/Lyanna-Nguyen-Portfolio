01 · Overview

Students are eager to join the club’s dino hunts, but get lost in the rules before they start

UC Davis Triceratops runs crochet dinosaur scavenger hunts across campus, and plenty of students want to join. But the rules, hunt details and first steps were scattered across a 41-channel Discord, so curious students got lost and gave up before their first hunt.

The club challenged our team of 6 to build its first website and turn that interest into members.

My contributions
  • Owned key parts of Getting Started and Flash Sales, turning scattered Discord rules into one clear path for new members.
  • Refined the new member journey from first interest to first hunt, ordering content by when members need it to reduce user friction.
  • Built my sections with Claude Code and the Figma MCP, QA’ing every breakpoint before launch.

02 · Problem

Joining meant decoding hunts, drops and claiming with no one to guide you

Joining the club means learning how hunts, drops and claiming work, all inside a Discord with 41 channels. This can be difficult if it's your first time and no one walks you through it. Many students give up before their first hunt because they can't find the rules, the hunt details or the next step.

How might we

How might we give new members a clear, step-by-step path to their first hunt before they even open Discord?

03 · Research

Research pinpointed the moment curious students gave up

With no usage data, I ran a 62-response survey, 12 interviews and a SWOT of 9+ club sites. I used AI to summarize every interview recording, so I could stay present and ask follow-up questions instead of taking notes, then compare consistent summaries across all 12.

Why it matters: the interest was already there but got lost in unclear rules for taking part, so our goal was to bring clarity and remove friction from the new member’s journey, from first interest to first hunt.

04 · Design decisions

Each round of testing cut what overwhelmed and kept what guided

What didn’t work

1A wall of rules that buried newcomers

Box after box of bullet points
  • Every rule in one place
  • Dense bullet lists in box after box read like a rulebook
  • Mentors · Early hi-fiCalled the page congested
  • Usability test · Hi-fi V12 of 6 testers had scroll fatigue

2Every step’s GIF playing at once

  • Shows each step in motion
  • Four moving GIFs compete for attention
  • Usability test · Hi-fi V12 of 6 testers missed these steps while every GIF played

3Plain, identical drop cards

Common and rare cards look identical
  • Quick to scan at a glance
  • Not enough room for all the drop details the club provided
  • Common and rare drops looked the same, so nothing felt special

What I chose

1Distilled the rules into bite-sized do’s and don’ts

One rule per line, with a dino to show it
  • Illustrated do’s and don’ts replace walls of bullets
  • Short, scannable sections, so each rule is easy to find

2Swapped simultaneous GIFs for 4 still steps

  • One thing to look at at a time
  • Click a step to see exactly where to go

3Turned drop types into collector cards

  • Leans into the hunt’s game-like feel, with rare drops styled to feel special to collect
  • A collector book pop-up holds each drop’s full details without crowding the page
05 · Final design

Two pages that turn curious students into hunters

06 · Reflections

What this project taught me to do differently

  • Cutting beats explaining. The clearest page is usually the shortest one, so I now start from what a new member needs first and keep only what helps them act on it.
  • Check early, not at the end. Fixes cost the least in the first draft, so I now check contrast, motion and breakpoints as I design and build, and review AI-generated code with the same care as my own.
